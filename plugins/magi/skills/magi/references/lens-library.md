# Lens library

Candidate seats for a MAGI council. Not a menu to be used whole — pick the
few the proposition actually turns on. Each entry gives what the seat asks,
what evidence moves it, and when it earns a seat.

## Orthogonality test

Before seating a lens, check it against the ones already seated:

1. Would one piece of evidence move both seats in the same direction at the
   same time? If yes, they are one seat. Keep the sharper one.
2. Can you name a fact that would make this seat vote against while another
   seat votes for? If you cannot, the seat adds a vote but no information.
3. Does this seat have a source of evidence the others do not read — a
   different log, a different repository, the deadline calendar, a paper, a
   reviewer's actual words? A seat with no distinct evidence source is a
   restatement of another seat's reasoning.

A council of three orthogonal seats is worth more than five overlapping ones.
Adding seats that fail these tests inflates the tally without improving it.

## Evidence load-bearing

*Asks*: does the evidence this decision rests on actually support what it is
being asked to support? Which claim breaks if it does not?

*Moved by*: the original log or run output rather than a summary of it;
sample sizes; whether the number quoted is the number measured.

*Seat it when*: the proposition is justified by a prior result, or the work
being proposed is meant to produce evidence for a claim already written down.

## Internal validity and confounds

*Asks*: would the proposed work distinguish the hypothesis from its
alternatives, or would the result be consistent with several explanations?

*Moved by*: the design itself — controls, what is held fixed, what covaries
with what; prior null results and what they failed to rule out.

*Seat it when*: the decision is whether to run an experiment or a probe. This
seat is the one that catches work that would be done correctly and still
answer nothing.

## Cost and schedule

*Asks*: what does this consume — compute, person-hours, calendar — and what
does the deadline permit once that is subtracted?

*Moved by*: measured durations of comparable past runs, queue and hardware
constraints, the dates that cannot move.

*Seat it when*: almost always. It is the seat that keeps the council attached
to what is physically possible before the deadline.

## Opportunity cost and alternatives

*Asks*: what does this displace, and is there a cheaper thing that would
produce the same decision-relevant information?

*Moved by*: the list of what else is queued; a cheaper probe that would
resolve the same uncertainty.

*Seat it when*: the resource is genuinely contended, or when the proposal is
one of several routes to the same claim. Distinct from cost-and-schedule:
that seat asks whether it fits, this one asks whether it is the best use of
the space it takes.

## Outside evaluation

*Asks*: how does a reviewer, an advisor, or a reader receive this? Is it a
question that must be answered, or one nobody asked? Where will it be
attacked?

*Moved by*: actual reviewer comments, the venue's own criteria, what
comparable published work was required to show.

*Seat it when*: the decision affects something that leaves the group —
a submission, a talk, a report to a supervisor.

## Risk and reversibility

*Asks*: what is the worst outcome, and can it be undone? Does this consume
something that cannot be recovered — a submission slot, a subject pool, a
budget line, a collaborator's goodwill?

*Moved by*: whether an undo path exists and has been exercised; what the
irreversible step actually is.

*Seat it when*: the action is one-way, or touches money, people, or an
external party.

## Existing evidence: already answered?

*Asks*: has this question already been answered somewhere in the existing
record — an old log, a prior run, a paper — and the answer merely forgotten?

*Moved by*: the record itself. This seat's work is retrieval, not reasoning.

*Seat it when*: the work has a long history and the proposal sounds familiar.
Cheap to seat and occasionally it ends the council on its own.

## Data and statistical power

*Asks*: at the proposed scale, could the result distinguish the outcomes it
needs to distinguish? What effect size would be detectable?

*Moved by*: the variance of prior measurements, the number of conditions, the
comparison actually planned.

*Seat it when*: the proposal is a measurement or an evaluation and the
quantity is negotiable — a seat here often converts "do it or not" into "do
it at a size that would mean something".

## Ethics, compliance, and people

*Asks*: who is affected, what did they agree to, and what does the
institution require?

*Moved by*: the consent and recruitment terms, institutional rules, what was
promised to participants or collaborators.

*Seat it when*: human subjects, personal data, paid participants, or another
party's material is involved. This seat can also veto rather than vote — an
ethical bar is not outvoted by a majority, and when it reports a genuine
blocker the council reports a blocker, not a tally.
